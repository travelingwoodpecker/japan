Japan Central Alps · October 2026

Road signs

Japan signs in Japanese, with almost no English outside the expressways. These are the ones that actually change what you do.

Stop and slow — both are red triangles

止まれ

tomare

STOP. A full stop, wheels stationary. Enforced hard.

徐行

jokō

Slow — walking pace, ready to stop. Common on mountain roads.

進入禁止

shinnyū kinshi

Do not enter — you are facing a one-way street.

車両通行止め

sharyō tsūkōdome

Closed to all vehicles. Often seasonal on high passes.

二輪車通行止め

nirinsha tsūkōdome

No motorcycles. Red ring means prohibited — there is no diagonal slash.

Entrance and exit

入口

iriguchi

Entrance — the on-ramp onto an expressway, or the way in to a car park or service area. Usually paired with the interchange name.

出口

deguchi

Exit. On expressways it appears well ahead with the distance, then again at the off-ramp. Watch for it — miss one and the next is often far off.

Regulatory — blue means instruction, red ring means prohibition

最高速度

saikō sokudo

Speed limit in km/h. Numerals are blue, not black.

一方通行

ippō tsūkō

One way, in the direction of the arrow.

駐車禁止

chūsha kinshi

No parking. Two diagonals means no stopping at all.

国道

kokudō

National route marker — riders call it the onigiri. R292 is your Shibu Pass road.

Warning — yellow diamonds, all of these appear on your route

落石注意

rakuseki chūi

Falling rocks. Common after rain on the volcano roads.

連続カーブ

renzoku kābu

Continuous curves ahead.

すべりやすい

suberiyasui

Slippery when wet. Sulphur deposits near Shirane make this real.

動物注意

dōbutsu chūi

Animals. Deer and monkeys on the Nikkō and Shiga Kōgen roads at dusk.

Expressway services and rest stops

サービスエリア

sābisu eria

Service area. Fuel, hot food, shop, toilets — and almost always a marked motorcycle bay. Roughly every 50 km (30 mi).

パーキングエリア

pākingu eria

Parking area. Smaller — toilets, vending machines, sometimes a kiosk. Every 15 km or so, and often no fuel.

ETC専用

ETC senyō

Purple lane at a tollgate is ETC only. Green lanes are staffed. Approach slowly — the barrier lifts late.

道の駅

michi-no-eki

Roadside station on ordinary roads. Toilets, local food, and the most reliable stop once you leave the expressway.

Also worth knowing

Expressway guide signs are green, ordinary road signs are blue. A solid yellow centre line means no crossing and no overtaking; white dashes mean you may. 通行止 means the road is closed, and 冬季閉鎖 means closed for winter — both appear on gates at the foot of mountain roads. At tollgates, purple lanes are ETC only and green lanes are staffed.

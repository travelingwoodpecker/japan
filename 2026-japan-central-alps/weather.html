Weather & packing

October in Tōhoku is the good half of autumn — dry-ish, clear, the colour coming down the mountains to meet you. The catch is that every forecast number you will read is measured in a valley, and you will not be riding in the valley.

The one rule

Subtract 6 °C (11 °F) for every 1,000 m (3,300 ft) you climb — then another 4 °C (7 °F) for the wind.

Weather stations sit in towns, near the valley floor. Air cools about 6.5 °C (12 °F) for every 1,000 m (3,300 ft) of altitude, and riding at 80 km/h (50 mph) strips off roughly another 4–5 °C (7–9 °F) of apparent warmth. A forecast of 16 °C (61 °F) in Morioka is about 6 °C (43 °F) at the top of a 1,500 m (4,900 ft) pass, and feels like 1 °C (34 °F) through the vents. This is the gap that catches people out: they read the number, pack for the number, and freeze at the summit.

Ignore the 14-day forecast

Real forecast skill runs about seven days. Any site showing you a day-by-day number for two weeks out is generating a plausible-looking pattern, not predicting weather — and some serve stale data outright. Use the normals above for packing decisions, and wait for the real forecast to come into range before changing any plans.

Typhoons

Season runs May to October and peaks in August and September, so you are riding the tail end — but late-season storms tend to be stronger than early ones, and about three reach the main islands in a typical year. A mid-October typhoon is uncommon rather than rare. It is the one weather event that could force you to rework a day, and there is nothing to do about it now except know it exists and watch the forecast from about a week out.

When to check, and where

  • Around 1 October. First genuinely useful read on arrival and the opening riding days.
  • Around 7 October. The second half of the trip comes into forecast range. This is when a typhoon, if there is one, would show up.
  • Every evening on the road. Tomorrow's mountain weather, before you commit to a pass. Pair it with the road-closure links on the Bikes page — in October, closures are more likely than storms.

What to pack

One trip spans 21 °C (70 °F) Tokyo afternoons and near-freezing dawns at 1,100 m (3,600 ft). Layers solve that; a single warm jacket does not.

On the bike

  • A three-season jacket with the thermal liner. Not a mesh summer jacket. The liner comes out for the Tokyo end and goes back in for Mikuni and Hachimantai.
  • Two pairs of gloves. One warm, one lighter — and critically, so that a soaked pair has a day to dry. Wet gloves on a cold morning ruin a good road.
  • A neck tube. The highest warmth-per-gram item you can carry. A cold draught at the collar undoes a good jacket.
  • A one-piece rain suit that goes on over everything, plus waterproof overgloves. Better than trusting a jacket's own membrane, and far quicker at the roadside.
  • Waterproof boots. Boots that get soaked on 10/6 are still damp on 10/9. This is the item people regret.
  • A Pinlock or anti-fog visor insert. Cool mornings plus rain plus breath equals a fogged visor exactly when you need to see a wet apex.
  • Ask Ninja Tours about heated grips on Jenifer and Dobi. On the two cold mornings they are worth more than any layer.

Off the bike

  • Pack light for evenings. Every ryokan on this trip provides yukata, towels and toiletries. You need very little beyond a change of clothes and something for the feet.
  • Cash. Onsen towns and small restaurants are still often cash-only. 7-Eleven ATMs take foreign cards reliably.
  • Consider takuhaibin luggage forwarding. Send the city clothes ahead to the last Tokyo hotel instead of carrying them over eleven days of mountain roads.
  • Sunscreen. A low October sun at altitude, on a clear day, for six hours, with only your face exposed.
  • For the golf day: the club provides sets and shoes. Bring a glove and a windproof layer — an 8:59 tee time in the Hachimantai foothills is the coldest you will be standing still all trip.

The good news

October is one of the two best months to ride in Japan. The summer humidity has gone, the roads are dry more often than not, and the autumn colour is coming down off the high ground exactly as you cross it. Pack for a cold morning and a warm afternoon and you will spend eleven days in the best weather Tōhoku offers.
